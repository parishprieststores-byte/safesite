Emergency & Safety Guide

  1. Activate the nearest fire alarm pull station and alert people nearby.
  2. Evacuate via the nearest marked exit route — never use elevators.
  3. Stay low if there is smoke; check doors for heat before opening.
  4. Use a fire extinguisher (PASS method) only if the fire is small AND you are trained AND you have a clear escape path.
  5. Assemble at the designated assembly point and report to the roll-call warden.
  6. Never re-enter the building until officially cleared.
  1. Call for help and know your site's emergency number and first-aiders.
  2. Assess: Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation.
  3. Severe bleeding: apply firm direct pressure with a clean dressing.
  4. Burns: cool with running water for at least 20 minutes. Never apply ice or creams.
  5. Chemical splash to eye: flush at eyewash station for 15+ minutes.
  6. Do not move a casualty with suspected head, neck or back injury unless in immediate danger.
  1. Alert others and evacuate the immediate area if the spill is large or vapors are strong.
  2. Check the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the chemical before handling.
  3. Wear the PPE specified on the SDS (gloves, goggles, respirator as required).
  4. Contain with spill kit absorbents; never wash unknown chemicals down drains.
  5. Dispose of cleanup materials as hazardous waste; report the spill.
  1. Do not confront unauthorized persons — observe and report.
  2. Note: description, location, direction of travel, vehicle details.
  3. Call security / police. Give your name, location and what you see.
  4. Threat of violence: remove yourself, alert others discreetly, follow the site's lockdown or evacuation procedure.
  5. Active threat: Run if you can. Hide if you can't. Fight only as last resort. Silence phones.
  1. Never touch a person in contact with live electricity — isolate the power first.
  2. Use the electrical panel / emergency stop to cut power.
  3. Report shocks, even minor ones — they can cause delayed cardiac effects.
  4. Never use water on an electrical fire; use a CO2 or dry-powder extinguisher.
  • Head: hard hat where overhead hazards or falling objects exist.
  • Eyes: safety glasses minimum; goggles for chemicals/dust; face shield for grinding.
  • Hearing: earplugs/earmuffs when noise exceeds 85 dB (must raise voice to be heard at arm's length).
  • Hands: match glove type to hazard — cut, chemical, heat, electrical (never gloves near rotating machinery).
  • Feet: safety-toe footwear on site at all times.
  • High visibility: whenever vehicles or mobile plant operate nearby.
  • Fall protection: harness required at heights above the legal threshold (commonly 1.8–3 m / 6–10 ft).

The 11 checklists in this app are based on widely used safety frameworks, including OSHA general industry standards and ISO 45001 occupational health & safety management principles. They cover hazard identification, PPE, fire & emergency preparedness, electrical safety, machine guarding, ergonomics, chemical/HazCom, first aid, incident reporting, workplace security, and a monthly full-site audit.

Disclaimer: this app is a helpful tool, not a substitute for your local legal requirements, site-specific risk assessments, or professional advice. Always follow your jurisdiction's regulations.
